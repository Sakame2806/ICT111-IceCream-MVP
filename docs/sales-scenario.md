# Sales Scenario - IceCream

## 1. Project Title

**IceCream: A Campus Digital Art Community**

## 2. Target User / Customer

- **Primary users:** campus digital artists, illustrators, comic artists, and hobbyist creators.
- **Secondary users:** students who want to discover artwork by title, style, or fandom tag.

## 3. Situation / Context

A student illustrator finishes a multi-page character study and shares it in a busy class or club chat. Within a few hours, new messages push the post out of sight. Reactions are mostly emoji, the pages are no longer viewed in the intended order, and students interested in the same fandom cannot find the work later.

During an art-club showcase, the team introduces IceCream on a laptop and mobile device. The student opens the landing page, creates an alias-based account, uploads the pages in order, adds a title and tags, and publishes the work to a searchable gallery.

## 4. Pain Point

Existing chat feeds are fast-moving and poorly organized for visual portfolios. Artists experience:

- short-lived visibility;
- shallow or unstructured reactions;
- difficulty presenting multi-page work in sequence;
- weak discovery by style or fandom;
- fragmented personal portfolios.

## 5. Product Introduction

IceCream is introduced as a focused local web prototype for publishing and discovering campus artwork. It provides an artwork gallery, multi-image upload, title/tag search, artwork detail pages, profiles, likes, and a creator dashboard.

## 6. Value Message

For campus digital artists and hobbyists whose work gets buried in busy chat feeds, IceCream helps their artwork stay organized and discoverable through multi-image publishing, searchable tags, dedicated artwork pages, and creator profiles.

## 7. Sales Conversation / Pitch Moment

**Team:** "When you share artwork in a class chat, how long can people still find it?"

**Student artist:** "Usually only until the next conversation starts."

**Team:** "IceCream gives that work a permanent place in a campus-focused gallery. You can upload several pages in order, label the work with fandom or style tags, and keep every upload together on your profile. Other students can search the title or tag instead of scrolling through old chat messages."

**Student artist:** "Can I see whether people are interested in the work?"

**Team:** "Yes. The prototype displays likes and a creator dashboard, so you can see interest in your work and keep your uploads together."

## 8. Expected User Action

1. Start the local prototype server.
2. Explore the gallery.
3. Create an alias-based account.
4. Upload a test artwork with appropriate tags.
5. Search for a related artwork.
6. Open an artwork detail page and give usability feedback.
7. Share the prototype with other campus artists.

## 9. Related Requirements

- **FR-01, FR-12, FR-13:** clear, consistent, responsive entry experience.
- **FR-02, FR-09, FR-10:** account-to-upload workflow, validation, and feedback.
- **FR-03, FR-04, FR-05:** stored artwork records, gallery, and search/tag discovery.
- **FR-04, FR-12:** artwork detail and consistent navigation experience.
- **FR-11:** creator dashboard and engagement summary.
- **FR-14:** aliases and responsible data handling.
- **FR-15:** traceability from requirements to screens and demo steps.
